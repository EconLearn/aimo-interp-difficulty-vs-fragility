PRE-REGISTRATION: permutation null for the 'same label' claim (draft 36 of the four-page report)
Written 2026-10-22 ~08:30 UTC, BEFORE any permutation is computed. Its md5 is recorded in PREREG_samelabel.txt.md5 before
perm_samelabel.py is first run; the script prints this file's md5 and refuses to run if it differs.

WHY. Draft 35, Section 4, says the labels "look like a property of the problem, not the model": of the 16 public problems
labelled for more than one configuration, 15 carry one label, and 4 of the 5 with a non-robust label are non-robust for
every configuration (80 labelled cases by exact text; 14 of 15 and 3 of 4 on the 71 cases of the 82-row file). A cold
reader (ledger 682) asked for a null: with about 78% of labels robust, many problems would carry one label (all robust)
by chance. This test asks whether 15 of 16 is more than each configuration's robust share alone would give.

STATISTICS (on the groups whose rows span >= 2 configurations, "multi"):
  S1 = number of multi groups whose rows all carry one label (all robust or all non-robust).
  S2 = among multi groups with at least one non-robust label, the share whose labels are all non-robust.

NULL. Labels are shuffled across problems WITHIN each configuration: for each of the seven configurations, the labels of
that configuration's rows in the case set are randomly permuted among those rows (all its labelled rows, including rows
on problems labelled for one configuration only). Each configuration's robust share is kept exactly; the grouping of rows
into problems is fixed. 10,000 permutations, numpy.random.default_rng(20261022), configurations in the order Qwen,
Skywork, Olmo, DeepSeek, high, low, medium; LAB80 first, then LAB71 with a fresh generator on the same seed.

P-VALUES (one-sided).
  p1 = share of the 10,000 permutations with S1 >= the observed S1 (the brief's P(count >= observed)); also reported:
       (1 + that number) / 10,001.
  p2 = share of permutations with S2 >= the observed S2 (tolerance 1e-12). A permutation in which no multi group has a
       non-robust label has no S2; it counts as NOT reaching the observed share (primary); the share conditional on S2
       being defined is reported beside it.
  Also reported: the null mean and 5th / 95th percentiles of S1 and S2, and the number of permutations with S2 undefined.
  PRIMARY: p1 on LAB80 (the definition behind the paper's "15 of the 16"). SECONDARY: p1 on LAB71; p2 on both.
  Information only (no decision): the same on the 63 instance-matched rows of the 71 (reported, not used for wording).

DECISION RULE FOR THE PAPER'S WORDING (fixed now; applies to the primary p1, and separately to p2 for the 4-of-5 clause):
  p <= 0.05          : the claim stands, stated with its p ("more than each configuration's robust share gives by chance,
                       permutation p = ..."). Still post hoc, still few problems; the caveats stay.
  0.05 < p <= 0.20   : WEAKENED: "somewhat more than chance (p = ...)", "weak evidence"; "look like a property of the
                       problem" becomes "lean towards the problem".
  p > 0.20           : NOT SHOWN: the text must say that one label on 15 of 16 problems is about what the robust shares
                       alone give (null mean stated), and the problem-level reading rests only on what survives (S2, if
                       its p <= 0.20) or is dropped to "consistent with, not evidence for".
  The abstract follows the same branch. Whatever the result, it is reported in Section 4 with the null mean and p, and
  in CHANGES_v36.md; a reversal is reported as a reversal.

KNOWN LIMITS (stated now). The null keeps each configuration's robust share but not any link between label and problem
difficulty: if hard problems are non-robust for every model because they are hard, a small p does not separate
"fragility of the problem" from "difficulty of the problem" (draft 35's own caveat, harsher perturbation of some
problems, also remains). The groups are few (16 / 15), so p is coarse. Single analysis, no other statistic will be
substituted after seeing the result.
