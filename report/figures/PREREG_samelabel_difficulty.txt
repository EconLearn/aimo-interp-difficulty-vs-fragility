and the intervals of draft 37 of the four-page report
Written 2026-10-22 ~09:40 UTC, BEFORE any of the computations below is run. Its md5 is recorded in

WHY. Draft 36, Section 4, says labels follow the problem: of the 16 public problems labelled for several configurations,
15 get the same label from every configuration, against a mean of 5.88 when each configuration's labels are shuffled
across problems (perm_samelabel.out). A fourth cold reader (ledger 684) points out that this null destroys problem
DIFFICULTY, which is problem-level by the paper's own story (easy problems are robust for every model), so difficulty
alone would beat it, and the title's 'not fragility' is asserted, not tested. Draft 36's post hoc hard / easy stratified
null only partly answers this (two strata). The test below keeps difficulty, measured the way the paper measures it.
Definition used throughout: FRAGILITY = the problem-level part of the label that agreement does not explain.

(a) PRIMARY TEST: shuffle within configuration x difficulty bin.
  Bins: tertiles of A over the distinct problems of the case set being tested (each problem counted once, by its
  template's A): cut points q1, q2 = numpy.quantile(values, [1/3, 2/3]) (default linear method); bin 0 if A <= q1,
  bin 1 if q1 < A <= q2, bin 2 if A > q2. Bins are computed separately for LAB80 and for LAB71, each on its own problems.
  Null: for each cell (configuration, bin), the labels of that cell's rows (all its labelled rows, including rows on
  problems labelled for one configuration only) are randomly permuted among those rows. Each configuration's label count
  within each difficulty tertile is kept exactly; the grouping of rows into problems is fixed. 10,000 permutations,
  numpy.random.default_rng(20261022), cells in the order configuration (Qwen, Skywork, Olmo, DeepSeek, high, low, medium)
  then bin (0, 1, 2); LAB80 first, then LAB71 with a fresh generator on the same seed.
  Statistics (on the multi problems):
    S1 = number of multi problems whose rows all carry one label (all robust or all non-robust);
    S2 = among multi problems with at least one non-robust label, the share whose labels are all non-robust.
  p-values (one-sided): p1 = share of the 10,000 permutations with S1 >= observed S1; also reported (1 + n) / 10,001.
    p2 = share with S2 >= observed S2 (tolerance 1e-12); a permutation with no multi problem carrying an NR label has no
    S2 and counts as NOT reaching the observed share; the share conditional on S2 defined is reported beside it.
  Also reported: null mean and 5th / 95th percentiles of S1, the null mean of S2, the bin cut points, the problems per
  bin, and each cell's (rows, NR).
  PRIMARY: p1 on LAB80 (the paper's 15 of 16). Reported beside it: p1 on LAB71 (14 of 15); p2 on both.

  WORDING RULE (fixed now). The branch is the WEAKER of the branches of p1 on LAB80 and p1 on LAB71, so the choice of

(b) SECONDARY CHECK: does the other configurations' label on the same problem predict this configuration's label beyond
  agreement? statsmodels is not installed in any local venv (checked 2026-10-22: ModuleNotFoundError in aimo-hf, aimo,
  l2d, l2d-upstream); nothing will be installed, so no mixed or conditional logistic model is fitted. Instead:
  Rows: the rows of the multi problems (each such row has >= 1 other labelled configuration on its exact text); LAB80
  (primary for this check) and LAB71 (reported).
  Features: x1 = A(T) (this case's deployed agreement); x2 = the mean label (1 = non-robust) of the OTHER
  configurations' labelled rows on the same exact text (leave-one-configuration-out).
  Models: M0 = logistic(label ~ x1); M1 = logistic(label ~ x1 + x2). Intercept unpenalised; features standardised with
  the training fold's mean and SD (ddof 0; SD 0 -> 1); L2 penalty 0.5 * 1.0 * ||beta||^2 on the non-intercept
  coefficients (keeps separated folds finite); Newton-Raphson from zero, at most 100 steps, stop when the step's max
  absolute change < 1e-10.
  Leave-one-template-out: for each template T among the rows, fit on rows of other templates and predict the rows of T.
  (x2 of a held-out row uses the other configurations' labels on its own text: this is Appendix A's label-peeking oracle
  made into a test; coefficients never see the held-out template.)
  Metrics on the LOTO predictions: within-configuration AUC (the paper's metric; ties count half) and accuracy (call
  non-robust iff predicted probability >= 0.5), for M0 and M1, and the differences M1 - M0.
  Interval: template bootstrap of the evaluation rows (resample templates with replacement; predictions fixed), 10,000
  resamples, numpy.random.default_rng(20261022); 95% percentile interval; resamples with no within-configuration pair
  are dropped and counted.
  Reading: the check SUPPORTS a problem-level component beyond agreement if the 95% interval of the AUC difference M1 - M0
  on LAB80 lies above 0; otherwise NOT SHOWN at this size. The secondary check does not change the title (the primary
  rule does); it is reported in Section 4 (or Appendix A) with its interval, and if it disagrees with the primary test
  the text says so.

KNOWN LIMITS (stated now). Tertile bins are coarse: difficulty finer than a tertile, or difficulty that agreement does not
measure, can still produce clustering (by the definition above that residual counts as 'fragility', which is why the
secondary check conditions on agreement as a continuous value). A mismatched text gets its template's agreement. The
problems are few (16 / 15 multi), so p is coarse. If some problems were perturbed harder by the organisers, labels would
cluster by problem too; no test here separates that from fragility. Single analysis per item; no other statistic, bin
rule or seed will be substituted after seeing the results.
