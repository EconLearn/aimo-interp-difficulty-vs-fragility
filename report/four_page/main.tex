\title{Difficulty is not fragility: an audited robustness predictor and 88 negative results}

\author{\textbf{Jude Wallis (Independent researcher)}}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
We predict, inside a fixed GPU budget, whether a configuration's accuracy on a math problem survives the organisers'
perturbations, and find that difficulty is not fragility. On the 63 labelled public rows matching the text we read (all 71 in brackets), a stronger model's answer agreement ranks rows at within-configuration AUC 0.914 [0.929], mostly because 30 of 31
easy-template rows are robust; on hard templates it is 0.821 [0.875], and 2.5 times more attempts or a perfect answer
key raise it to at most 0.897. What it misses is mostly fragility of the
problem itself: 15 of 16 problems labelled for several configurations carry one label. No in-budget reading of that
from the original passed all its pre-set bars: of 88 negative results, 70 failed and 2 were unclear.
Leave-one-template-out our entry gets 55 of 63 [60 of 71] public and 17 of 22 [18 of 24] fresh rows, a selection null
cannot tell its board-chosen layer's gain from coin flips, and a 21-case validation board total varies by about 1.3
cases across server states.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The AIMO Interpretability Challenge \citep{stefanik2026} asks, for each \emph{case}, a (problem, configuration) pair,
whether the configuration's accuracy survives perturbation of the problem. The configurations are Qwen3.5-4B,
Skywork-OR1-Math-7B, Olmo-3-7B-Think and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (the small track), and gpt-oss-120b at low, medium
and high effort. A case is robust iff the largest accuracy drop over the organisers' families of perturbed texts is at
most 0.10, non-robust iff at least 0.25, and exists only if the configuration solves the original robustly. An entry
sees only the original text, runs each case in its own process on one GPU and faces 30 to 50 new problems in the final
at about 170\,s each.

We find that \textbf{difficulty is not fragility}: a stronger model's reading of difficulty carries most of the public
ranking, but what it misses on hard problems is mostly fragility of the problem itself, and no in-budget reading of that
from the original passed all its pre-set bars (Sections~\ref{sec:label} and~\ref{sec:neg}). Edited or re-instantiated
problems are known to cut accuracy \citep{mirzadeh2025,huang2025a,mondorf2026}; whether probes and interpretations
predict unseen behaviour is asked by \citet{belinkov2022}, \citet{li2025} and \citet{huang2025b}. We ask it of a whole
entry, with a selection null from adaptive data analysis \citep{dwork2015,blum2015,roelofs2019}.

\section{Data and the entry}
\label{sec:data}

\textbf{Data.} 82 public rows over 28 templates, 71 labelled (52 robust); \emph{fresh rows} are the 24 labelled rows on
12 AIME-2026 templates; 9 of 12 Skywork rows added on 15 October are labelled. \textbf{8 of the 71 labelled rows (9 of 80) carry other numbers than the text all our readings used}; we report the 63
\emph{matched} rows, with all rows in square brackets. The validation boards hold 21 main cases (10 robust) and 14 small cases (7
robust). As the site invites, we iterated on them with public-row-motivated method variants, never constant answers
per configuration or per-case probes, though early ones tested guesses about the hidden sets'
structure (Supplement E.6.2). Board counts per configuration carry hidden-label information, which we used while
choosing three routes (Supplement A.2); Section~\ref{sec:audit} audits that use.

\textbf{The entry reads behaviour only.} \emph{Stand-in} (Qwen, DeepSeek, gpt-oss low and medium cases): gpt-oss-120b at
low effort makes 4 to 32 attempts. Their \emph{agreement} (the chance that two random attempts both end on their own with the same answer), a
\emph{quick finish} (whether any of 4 non-thinking Qwen3.5-4B attempts ends within 2,000 tokens) and an effort code enter a logistic rule with 10 coefficients (\texttt{decision.json}; Supplement F.4).
\emph{Own runs} (Skywork, Olmo): robust iff at least 1 (Skywork) or 2 (Olmo) of 3 attempts of the judged model end on
their own inside a 9,000- or 10,000-token cap. \emph{Prior} (gpt-oss high): robust, as 13 of 15 public rows are.
\emph{The entry} is v95 (main) and v96 (small), rebuilt for the final as v111 and v112; this paper's evaluated entries
are their per-case-safe variants v115 and v116 (Codabench 1003729, 1003727; Section~\ref{sec:eff}), which cut Olmo's cap to 7,200 tokens
(Appendix~\ref{app:supp}).

\section{Difficulty is not fragility}
\label{sec:label}

\textbf{Two meanings of difficulty.} The judged model's solve rate carries almost none of the label: in our fit of the
label recipe the chance of a non-robust label is flat, 0.18 to 0.21, for solve rates from 0.5 to 1.0 (computed); on T13 every small model solves the problem yet is labelled non-robust. Difficulty read by a stronger model carries
far more: the stand-in's agreement at K~32 ranks matched rows at within-configuration AUC 0.914 [0.929].

\textbf{That reading saturates on hard templates} (Figure~\ref{fig:agree}; computed, post hoc, one server read). Its AUC
comes mostly from the 10 easy templates, where 30 of 31 rows are robust. On the 17 \emph{hard} templates,
which a 2,000-token reader never finishes (32 rows, 13 non-robust), it is 0.821 [0.875]: 7 of 39 [7 of 56]
same-configuration pairs are misordered (four on gpt-oss high, two on low, one on Skywork). 80 pooled attempts give 0.897 [0.929] and their accuracy against the answer key 0.872
[0.911], inside the 0.821 to 0.936 of single held reads; leave-one-template-out (LOTO), the key fixes
1 of 32 hard rows [2 of 37].

\textbf{What it misses is mostly the problem's own fragility} (computed, post hoc). By exact problem text (all 80 labelled rows), 15 of the 16 problems labelled for several configurations carry one label, and 4 of the 5 with
a non-robust label are non-robust for every configuration (T10 is the exception). An oracle calling a row non-robust when the other configurations' mean drop on the same text is at least 0.175 would fix 6.4 of our calls per read and break 0.4
(matched rows, 8 reads). These are few problems; the oracle uses labels.

\textbf{What fragility can look like.} On two harsher expert-style rewrites of T10, gpt-oss low's wrong answers are exactly
those of the correct method without one check, a lower triangle bound; at the original numbers the check removes
nothing (both give 850), so the original cannot show the slip (an equivalent mutant; \citealp{papadakis2015}).
gpt-oss medium makes the same slip yet is labelled robust, so our rewrites are harsher than the organisers'.

\textbf{The board-chosen layer against a selection null.} Keeping a change only when the board rises gains +3.9 in
expectation (90\% range 1 to 8) if every changed answer is a coin flip and +7.3 (3 to 12) if each is right 70\% of the

cannot separate them, and \textbf{the board-chosen layer adds no demonstrable accuracy}.

\textbf{A frozen prediction.} We expect v111 to get \textbf{about 16 of 21 main cases right on new problems} (90\% range
11 to 20), not our best draw's 19. Guess ranges for final accuracy, frozen on 17 October (assumptions, hash and their
rule for the evaluated v115 and v116: Appendix~\ref{app:supp}), are 0.72 to 0.82 for v111 and 0.62 to 0.76 for v112;
Section~\ref{sec:noise}'s noise alone would put the final inside them only about 40 to 50\% (v111) and 55 to 70\%
(v112) of the time (computed).

\section{How much a board score moves}
\label{sec:noise}

From our own logs, without hidden labels (computed): a reading repeats exactly within one server state (23 of 23
same-problem groups), but across states a stand-in call flips with probability about 0.26 (90\% interval 0.14 to 0.45).
One board total thus varies by about 1.3 cases on main and 1.0 on small. \textbf{A 21-case board cannot order entries whose readings resample and that differ by two cases or
fewer}: 17 against 15 arises with probability 0.41 with no real difference. On 30 to 50 new problems, reading noise and
problem sampling give about 0.07 to 0.09 accuracy per run.

\section{Efficiency under a \texorpdfstring{170\,s}{170 s} per-case budget}
\label{sec:eff}

Our decode paths share K/V reads across a KV head's query heads and write K and V in place; in pre-registered server
benches a decode run takes 0.42x (Skywork) and 0.48x (Olmo) of the default wall time. v115 and v116 answered every board case in at most 148\,s, under the final's 170\,s.

\section{Limitations and lessons}
\label{sec:limits}

\textbf{Limitations.} Reading noise rests on 3 server states; fresh rows were seen while hand-set values
were chosen; the own runs rest on thin, post hoc support (3 Skywork and 9 Olmo rows when chosen); a Claude agent wrote
the rating rubric after seeing the labels; raters and blind writers are Claude subagents; the final is mocked, not run.
\textbf{Lessons.} Check that each labelled row's text is the text you read; split a reading's score by where the label
varies; refit label-fitted parts LOTO and compare board gains with a selection null (\texttt{audit.py}); set bars above
read-to-read noise.
