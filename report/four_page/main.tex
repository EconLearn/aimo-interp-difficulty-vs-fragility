\title{Answer agreement measures difficulty, not fragility: a robustness predictor and its self-audit}

\author{\textbf{Jude Wallis (Independent researcher)}}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The AIMO Interpretability Challenge asks an entry to predict, from a math problem's original text, whether a model's
accuracy survives the organisers' perturbations. We ask what a working robustness predictor reads. Ours mostly reads how
often the repeated answers of a proxy model, gpt-oss-120b at low effort, agree: on 63 labelled public cases, agreement
alone ranks robust above non-robust cases within each configuration with AUC 0.914, mostly because 30 of 31 cases on
easy problem templates are robust; on hard ones it is 0.821. Pooled over six other reads, agreement and the proxy's
answer-key accuracy rank hard cases about equally, 0.897 against 0.872, one pair in 39 apart, within noise: agreement
measures difficulty. Labels cluster by problem text: of 16 texts labelled for several configurations, 15 get one label
from every configuration, against about 9 when labels are shuffled among texts of similar agreement, $p \leq 0.0001$ on
80 cases, and 14 of 15 against 10.30, $p = 0.0035$, on the 71 labelled first; harsher perturbation of some texts would do
the same. The entry is right on 55 of 63 public cases when refitted without each template, and on 15 of 21 main and 9
of 14 small hidden validation cases, against 49, 10 and 7 for always answering robust; none of the three is
distinguishable from it, and the small track fell short of the 12.2 expected from public cases, a model-based value
computed after the boards. Our self-audit finds that rules kept because the validation score rose add no demonstrable
accuracy, that 26\% of agreement-based calls flip across days, and that the score's rerun standard deviation is about
1.3 cases.
\end{abstract}

\section{The question and our answer}
\label{sec:intro}

The AIMO Interpretability Challenge \citep{stefanik2026} labels each pairing of a math problem with a model
configuration as robust or non-robust, by how far its accuracy falls on the organisers' perturbations. Seven
configurations are judged: Qwen3.5-4B, Skywork-OR1-Math-7B, Olmo-3-7B-Think and DeepSeek-R1-0528-Qwen3-8B (the small
track) and, on the main track, also gpt-oss-120b at low, medium and high effort. An entry sees only the original
problem and in the final meets 30 to 50 new problems at about 170\,s each. Perturbed problems cut accuracy
\citep{mirzadeh2025,huang2025a,mondorf2026}; whether interpretations predict unseen behaviour is asked by
\citet{belinkov2022}, \citet{li2025} and \citet{huang2025b}; agreement among sampled answers is a known signal of
correctness and difficulty \citep{wang2023,kuhn2023,farquhar2024}, as is a model's estimate of what it knows
\citep{kadavath2022}. Our interpretability question is what a working robustness predictor reads. \textbf{Our answer:
how hard the problem is (Section~\ref{sec:label}); what it misses looks like \emph{fragility}, the problem-level part
of the label that agreement does not explain (Section~\ref{sec:miss}), which no reading we tried caught well enough to
pass all its pre-set bars (Section~\ref{sec:neg}).} New here are this split and the audit of our own entry
(Section~\ref{sec:audit}).

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:data}

\textbf{Data.} We ran one text per template; 8 of the 71 labelled public cases were labelled on different numbers, so we
drop them and keep 63. All our public-case numbers use these 63 (49 robust) unless noted; Appendix~\ref{app:supp} gives every value on all labelled cases. 22 of the 63, on 12 AIME-2026 templates, are
\emph{newer cases}; they are not held out: we saw them while choosing hand-set values.

\textbf{The entry} reads only behaviour on the original. For Qwen, DeepSeek and gpt-oss low and medium cases, the
proxy model makes K attempts (K = 4 to 32), and a logistic rule fitted to public labels calls the case from their
agreement, whether any of 4 non-thinking Qwen3.5-4B attempts ends within 2,000 tokens, and whether the case is gpt-oss
medium. For Skywork and Olmo cases it runs the judged model (\emph{own runs}): robust iff at least 1 (Skywork) or 2
(Olmo) of 3 attempts, or 1 if time allows fewer, end inside a 9,000- or 10,000-token cap (the evaluated entries cut
Olmo's cap to 7,200 and stop own runs at 160\,s; public-case numbers apply these rules to one K 32 read, with 3 Olmo
attempts on 5 of 28 templates; Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). gpt-oss high cases are always called robust.

\section{What agreement reads: difficulty}
\label{sec:label}

\textbf{The test} (post hoc; interval and wording rule pre-set). If agreement captured more than difficulty, it should
clearly beat a pure difficulty score, the proxy's answer-key accuracy. A ranking's \emph{within-configuration AUC} is
the share of same-configuration (robust, non-robust) pairs it orders correctly; 95\% intervals resample templates. A
\emph{read} is one set of K attempts per template in a server run. On hard cases, over the same 80 attempts of six
reads, agreement beats accuracy only marginally, within noise: 0.897 against 0.872 (difference 0.025, interval 0.000
to 0.176); swapping that accuracy in for the K 32 read's agreement, refitting without each template, changes 1 of 32
hard-case calls, from wrong to right. Agreement measures difficulty. The judged model's solve rate predicts little: in
a simulation of the organisers' labelling, a case is non-robust with probability 0.18 to 0.21 whether it solved the
original half the time or always (model-based), as its non-robust labels come from variants breaking a solving model
(every small model solves T13, yet all four are non-robust); agreement, where unfinished attempts never agree, may
grade the work a problem needs (our hypothesis).

\textbf{The headline numbers} (post hoc). One read at K = 32, by an earlier build of the entry (which uses 4 to 32
attempts as time allows), gives agreement AUC 0.914 (0.778 to 0.993) on all 105 pairs: our headline, a
score of the agreement feature, not of the entry. Most of it comes from the 10 easy
templates, where 30 of 31 cases are robust. On hard templates this read gives 0.821 (0.448 to 1.000), with 7 of 39
pairs misordered, tying the lowest of the six smaller reads (0.821 to 0.936), and their 80 pooled attempts (0.897) fall
in that range too: more attempts do not clearly help; all rest on 39 pairs.

\textbf{What agreement cannot see: T10}, a geometry problem summing a triangle's possible side lengths, illustrates how
answer-key checks can miss a slip. gpt-oss low's agreement is higher on its non-robust T10 (0.762) than on its robust
T09 (0.655). On two harsher rewrites, its wrong answers are those of the correct method minus one check, a lower
triangle bound; at the original numbers the check removes nothing (both give 850). The condition is \emph{dormant}, and
the slip an \emph{equivalent mutant}, a change invisible on the tested input \citep{papadakis2015}. It was seen in our
harsher rewrites only (gpt-oss medium makes it yet is robust), not shown to cause the organisers' label.

\section{What agreement misses: the problem's own fragility}
\label{sec:miss}

Labels follow the exact problem text, not the model (computed, post hoc): of 16 problem texts labelled for several
configurations, 15 get one label from every configuration, and 4 of the 5 with a non-robust label are non-robust for
all; T10 is the exception. Reading no output of ours, this count uses all 80 labelled cases, 9 labelled after our
design. \textbf{Difficulty, as thirds of agreement measure it, does not explain it.} In a test we pre-registered, that
is, wrote in our dated records before the result existed (no external timestamp), shuffling each configuration's
labels 10,000 times among problem texts in the same third of agreement gives one label on 9.43 of the 16 on average,
never 15 ($p \leq 0.0001$), and never 4 of 5 all non-robust; on the 71 cases labelled first, 35 shuffles reach 14 of
15 (null mean 10.30, $p = 0.0035$). By our pre-set rule, the claim stands beyond difficulty. A second pre-registered
check, adding other configurations' labels on the same text to agreement, points the same way but is not significant
(AUC gain 0.094; Appendix~\ref{app:supp}). Caveats: few texts; finer or unseen difficulty counts here as fragility;
harsher perturbation of some texts would do the same.

\section{How well the entry generalises}
\label{sec:audit}
\label{sec:noise}

\textbf{Against always answering robust} (Table~\ref{tab:audit}). Refitted without each template, the entry is right
on 55 of 63 public cases (always robust: 49) and on 17 of the 22 newer cases (12). The main board: 15 of 21 (always
robust: 10), inside the expected range (earlier builds: 16, 17). \textbf{The small track fell short}: 9 of 14 (earlier
builds: 10, 9) against 12.2 expected from public cases (model-based, computed after the boards; all cases: 11.4, 8 to
14; always robust: 7). \textbf{None is distinguishable from always robust.} A post hoc paired exact McNemar test gives
$p = 0.18$ on public cases (newer: also 0.18). Boards report totals; +5 on main needs at least 5 non-robust calls, and
even 5 right of 5 gives a two-sided sign-test $p$ of 0.0625; the small track's 9 of 14 is not distinguishable from
always robust's 7 on its own ($p$ at least 0.5). Earlier builds' +6 and +7 allow 0.031 and 0.016, which totals cannot
settle.

\textbf{What was fitted, and when.} Only the logistic rule's coefficients were fitted to the public labels, on 5
October: after uploads began (2 October), before the board-tested changes of 5 to 11 October. About ten hand-set values,
among them the own-run thresholds and the gpt-oss high constant (chosen from all public labels: robust 13 times in 15),
were set with labels in view and escape cross-validation. Uploads tested variants with a public-case reason, never
per-case probes, but per-configuration board counts guided which to try (Supplement A.2), and early ones tested guesses
about the hidden sets (Supplement E.6.2). Three rules tried there, the
gpt-oss high constant and the Skywork and Olmo own-run rules, decide 8 of 21 main and 6 of 14 small cases; the board did
not rise with the constant, and the own-run rules were kept because the board rose (+3 in all).

\textbf{Did board feedback help?} If changed answers are coin flips, keeping only changes that raise the board still
gains +3.9 cases on average (90\% range 1 to 8); if each is right 70\% of the time, +7.3 (3 to 12) (model-based). The
kept +3 lies inside both, so this \emph{selection null} \citep{dwork2015,blum2015,roelofs2019} cannot tell them apart:
\textbf{the rules kept because the board rose add no demonstrable accuracy.} We keep them because dropping them is not
better supported: on 9 Skywork cases labelled after the choice, the own run beats the proxy (mean 8.0 against 7.25
right; one on other numbers), and the pre-registered switch rule and a later review kept it, though the switch gains
in 4 of 5 reads on the cases labelled first and passed one pre-registered bench gate.

\textbf{Calls are not reproducible across days} (computed). With fixed seeds, same-day reruns match exactly (23 of 23
checks), but in another \emph{server state}, 26\% of agreement-based calls flip (90\% interval 0.14 to 0.45); own-run
and always-robust calls never did. \textbf{This is a reproducibility limit of the entry, and its cause is unknown}. One
board total has a model-based standard deviation of about 1.3 cases on main and 1.0 on small, so a board cannot order
entries two or fewer cases apart.

\section{What did not work}
\label{sec:neg}

Of 88 negative results to 20 October (Supplement D), \textbf{70 failed a pre-registered bar}; 2 were unclear, 13 had
no or a post hoc bar, 3 were never run. Table~\ref{tab:neg} shows three of them; its first row, a 21 October
internal-state probe of the judged model, reads difficulty; Qwen's solve rate in its place loses cases, so \textbf{we
make no White-Box (internal-signal) claim}. No fragility reading passed its bars. Programs a Claude agent wrote after
reading raters' notes scored 12 of 12 Skywork and Olmo cases of Table~\ref{tab:neg}'s set, blind ones 9 to 11:
\textbf{a reading computed through a solver's own steps needs blind replication.}

\section{Lessons}
\label{sec:lessons}

Check labelled texts against the texts you read. Split a score by where labels vary; test problem-level claims with a
difficulty-keeping null; refit label-fitted parts leaving one template out; compare with baselines by paired tests, and
validation gains with a selection null. For benchmark builders: only numbers on which a dormant condition binds can tell
a method from its equivalent mutant.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

Reading noise rests on 3 server states; the newer cases are not held out; the own-run rules rest on thin, post hoc
support (3 Skywork and 9 Olmo cases when chosen); the raters, whose rubric a Claude agent wrote after seeing the labels,
and the blind writers are Claude subagents; pre-registration has no outside timestamp; the final is rehearsed on a mock,
not run (frozen forecast: Appendix~\ref{app:supp}).
